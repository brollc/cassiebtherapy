How I think about therapy

Therapy

I use the word work intentionally here. Therapy is many things, and effortful is one of them. It is not easy to sit in places inside ourselves and in emotions we try hard to avoid, to look at things we usually turn away from, to talk with our partner about a topic that has somehow, in some unspoken way, become off-limits. It's hard to believe that a process often described with words like healing, holding space, journey, discovery, growth can also be associated with tears, fear, anxiety, sadness, and grief. Ah, the human experience — full of paradoxes and complexity. Thank goodness I love both of those things!

I pull from a few different theories in my practice. Most notably, I take a narrative, psychodynamic, and somatic approach to therapy, all through an attachment framework. If you read that and are thinking, What on earth does that mean?! Awesome. Look at you, already asking great questions! Here's a little more about what each of those approaches means and how they might show up in our work together.

Narrative

The stories we tell ourselves are powerful. We all have stories about who we are, how the world works, and what we can expect from others. Some of those stories come from our families, our experiences, and the cultures we're part of. Together, we'll get curious about the stories you've been carrying, where they came from, and whether they're still serving you.

Psychodynamic

Our early experiences matter. Some of our work will involve exploring your early experiences, family of origin, and the ways those experiences continue to shape how you relate to yourself and others. We'll also pay attention to the emotions, expectations, and relational patterns that emerge in the therapy room itself. Sometimes, what happens between us can offer valuable insight into what happens in other relationships.

Somatic

Our body holds important knowledge and we need to be able to connect to it. What are you feeling in your body? Can you sense it? What happens in your body when you're overwhelmed, anxious, or triggered? Our bodies hold experiences and information that we don't always have words for. Through a somatic lens, we'll make room for that knowledge and explore how your nervous system responds to the world around you.

Attachment

Our relationships matter. This is the framework at the heart of my work. At its core is a simple but important idea: our relationships matter. The ways we've experienced connection, safety, and belonging shape how we understand ourselves and how we show up with others. And this doesn't stop with our early relationships — our partnerships, friendships, and even work relationships continue to influence us.

Questions we might sit with together:

  • Who have you learned you need to be in order to feel loved or accepted?
  • What do you believe others expect of you?
  • Do you find yourself performing, placating, withdrawing, or pushing others away when relationships feel difficult?
  • What happens when you need something from someone else?

These are just some of the questions we might explore together.

At the heart of it

A few of my beliefs in therapy

  • I believe the process of getting to know ourselves, meeting all parts of ourselves and our stories with compassion, and bringing all we are to our relationships in an authentic, intimate way is one of the most rewarding things we will ever do in life.
  • I believe therapy is sacred work.
  • I believe we need humor in life and also the therapy room.
  • I believe that when our relationship with our partner feels secure, loving, and embracing, we can face almost anything in life. When our relationship feels like we're walking on eggshells and covered in tension, the rest of our lives feels like crap too.
  • I believe that people are complex and inherently good.
